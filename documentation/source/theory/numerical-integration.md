# Numerical integration and singularities

{.mom-status}
{bdg-info}`Draft` Written from `solver/utils/impmet.py` and `solver/utils/quadrature/` at commit `1b6a6e2`, awaiting review.

Every entry of the impedance matrix is a sum of double surface integrals over pairs of triangles
(see {eq}`eq-zmn-discrete`). Their kernel $e^{-jkR}/R$ becomes infinite when the two points meet,
which happens only when the two triangles touch. The solver therefore sorts the pairs and integrates
each kind with a dedicated scheme.

## The idea

For two distant triangles the integrand is smooth and a few quadrature points per triangle give an
accurate result. When the triangles share an edge, a vertex, or are the same triangle, $R$ goes to
zero somewhere and a plain rule either loses accuracy or divides by zero. Those pairs are few (about
10 % on the mesh of {numref}`fig-ni-pairs`), so they can afford more careful, more expensive
treatments without slowing the fill much.

## Four kinds of triangle pairs

`singularity_classifier.classify_triangle_pair` compares the vertex indices of the test and source
triangles:

```{list-table}
:header-rows: 1
:widths: 14 30 56

* - Category
  - Shared vertices
  - Treatment in `impmet`
* - **FAR**
  - none
  - the same Gauss rule on both triangles (default degree 6, 12 points)
* - **VERTEX**
  - one
  - Gauss rule of degree 16 (81 points) on both triangles, $1/R$ part of the charge term in closed
    form on the source triangle
* - **EDGE**
  - two
  - same as VERTEX
* - **SELF**
  - three (same triangle)
  - Gauss rule on the test triangle (degree 6); around each of its nodes, Duffy transform on the
    source triangle (order 7, 147 nodes)
```

## Gauss rules on triangles

A rule of degree $d$ integrates exactly every polynomial of total degree $\le d$ on the triangle. The
nodes are given in barycentric coordinates and the weights sum to ½, so that for a triangle of area
$A$

```{math}
:label: eq-dunavant
\int_T g\,dS \;\approx\; 2A\sum_{q=1}^{N_q} w_q\, g(\mathbf r_q).
```

`gauss_dunavant.dunavant_rule(degree)` offers the degrees below. Degrees 1 to 6 are the symmetric
rules of {footcite:t}`dunavant1985`. **Degrees 7 and above are not Dunavant rules**: they are
Gauss–Legendre product rules mapped onto the triangle, because, as the module explains, the published
symmetric coefficients above degree 6 could not be transcribed to full double precision.

```{list-table}
:header-rows: 1
:widths: 20 20 60

* - Degree
  - Points
  - Type
* - 1, 3, 4, 5
  - 1, 4, 6, 7
  - symmetric Dunavant rules
* - **6** (FAR, SELF outer)
  - **12**
  - symmetric Dunavant rule
* - 7, 11
  - 25, 49
  - Gauss–Legendre product rules
* - **16** (EDGE/VERTEX)
  - **81**
  - Gauss–Legendre product rule
* - 20, 24
  - 121, 169
  - Gauss–Legendre product rules
```

## FAR pairs

The integrand of {eq}`eq-zmn-discrete` is smooth and the default degree-6 rule is applied on both
triangles. Because the same rule is used on both sides, exchanging the test and source triangles
gives exactly the same number: FAR entries are symmetric to machine precision.

## SELF pairs: the Duffy transform

When the test and source triangles are the same, every test node $\mathbf r_p$ lies inside the source
triangle and $1/|\mathbf r_p - \mathbf r'|$ is infinite at $\mathbf r' = \mathbf r_p$. The solver
keeps a Gauss rule on the test triangle and, for each of its nodes, integrates over the source
triangle with the **Duffy transform** {footcite:p}`duffy1982`:

1. the source triangle is split into three sub-triangles that share the singular point as a vertex
   (dashed lines in {numref}`fig-ni-pairs` d);
2. on each sub-triangle $(\mathbf q_0, \mathbf q_1, \mathbf q_2)$, with the singular point at
   $\mathbf q_0$, the coordinates are "collapsed":

```{math}
:label: eq-duffy
\mathbf r'(u,v) = \mathbf q_0 + u(\mathbf q_1 - \mathbf q_0) + uv(\mathbf q_2 - \mathbf q_1),
\qquad dS' = 2A_{\mathrm{sub}}\,u\,du\,dv,
\qquad (u,v)\in[0,1]^2 .
```

Since $|\mathbf r' - \mathbf q_0| = O(u)$, the factor $u$ of the area element cancels the $1/R$
singularity: the transformed integrand is bounded, and an ordinary Gauss–Legendre rule in $u$ and $v$
converges. The solver uses 7 points per direction (`duffy_order = 7`), that is 3 × 49 = 147 nodes per
outer node.

**A detail that matters.** The Duffy nodes come with weights summing to the physical area $A$, while
the rules of {eq}`eq-dunavant` sum to ½. The SELF branch mixes the two, so it rescales the Duffy
weights by $1/(2A)$ (`impmet._duffy_weight_scale`). Its docstring explains what happened when that
factor was missing: the SELF contributions were scaled by $2A$, i.e. almost removed, and
$R = \mathrm{Re}\,Z$ lost its positive semi-definiteness. `test_impedance_quadrature_normalisation.py`
now checks the invariant that would have caught it: scaling the geometry by $s$ and the frequency by
$1/s$ must leave the spectrum of $R$ unchanged.

## EDGE and VERTEX pairs: the singularity in closed form

When the triangles share an edge or a vertex, the Gauss nodes never coincide, but they come close to
each other along the shared part and $1/R$ varies too fast for a moderate rule. The solver handles
the dominant term, the charge term (larger than the current term by about $4/(kh)^2$), by splitting
its kernel:

```{math}
:label: eq-singularity-extraction
\frac{e^{-jkR}}{R} = \underbrace{\frac{e^{-jkR} - 1}{R}}_{\text{bounded, }\to\,-jk}
\;+\; \underbrace{\frac{1}{R}}_{\text{singular}} .
```

The bounded part is integrated with the degree-16 rule on both triangles. The singular part is
integrated over the **source triangle in closed form**, with the formula of
{footcite:t}`wilton1984`, and the result is integrated over the test triangle with the same rule.
For an observation point $\mathbf r_0$ at signed height $h$ above the plane of the source triangle:

```{math}
:label: eq-wilton
\begin{aligned}
\int_T \frac{dS'}{|\mathbf r_0 - \mathbf r'|}
= \sum_{i=1}^{3}\Bigg[\, & P_i^0 \ln\frac{R_i^+ + l_i^+}{R_i^- + l_i^-}\\
& - |h|\left(\arctan\frac{P_i^0\, l_i^+}{(R_i^0)^2 + |h|R_i^+}
- \arctan\frac{P_i^0\, l_i^-}{(R_i^0)^2 + |h|R_i^-}\right)\Bigg],
\end{aligned}
```

where, for edge $i$ going from vertex $\mathbf A$ to vertex $\mathbf B$: $P_i^0$ is the signed
distance from the projection of $\mathbf r_0$ to the line of the edge, $l_i^\mp$ the signed positions
of $\mathbf A$ and $\mathbf B$ along it, $R_i^\mp = |\mathbf A - \mathbf r_0|$,
$|\mathbf B - \mathbf r_0|$, and $(R_i^0)^2 = (P_i^0)^2 + h^2$. Because $P_i^0$ is signed, the
formula holds whether the projection falls inside the triangle or not. That is what EDGE and VERTEX
pairs need, and what a Duffy subdivision cannot give: it only tiles the triangle when the point is
inside it.

The $|h|$ term vanishes for coplanar triangles but not at a junction between two surfaces. The module
`analytic_singular` records what dropping it costs: on a 40 mm cell, 34 % error at a 90° junction.
`test/test_impedance_non_coplanar.py` keeps a permanent three-dimensional case. The current term keeps
the plain degree-16 rule: it is smaller by $(kh)^2/4$, so its residual error is negligible.

## How accurate is each scheme?

{numref}`fig-ni-convergence` isolates the static kernel $1/R$, the part that each special scheme
exists for, on an EDGE pair and a SELF pair of the mesh above, and compares it with a reference
accurate to about $10^{-6}$.

The same behaviour is documented in the code for complete matrix entries, measured during
development (comments in `impmet.py`):

- EDGE pairs: closed-form extraction took the error from +1.15 % (plain Gauss, degree 11) to
  −0.008 %;
- SELF pairs: 0.22 % at outer degree 6 on a 40 mm cell. Raising the outer degree alone makes the
  Duffy integral non-monotone (2.05 %, 0.008 %, 0.275 %, 0.005 % at Duffy orders 7, 10, 14, 25 with
  an outer degree 11), so `self_outer_order` and `duffy_order` must be raised **together** or not at
  all. The code comment names the way to lift this ceiling: replace the inner Duffy integral by the
  closed form, as the EDGE/VERTEX branch already does.

## Reciprocity as a health check

Only FAR pairs use the same rule on both sides; SELF and EDGE/VERTEX are numerical on the test
triangle and analytic (or Duffy) on the source. So a matrix filled with `fill_mode="full"` is not
exactly symmetric, and $\max|Z - Z^\mathsf T|/\max|Z|$ measures the quadrature error of the singular
branches. The docstring of `NEAR_ORDER_DEFAULT` gives that residual on the CubeSat mesh at 440 MHz
($N = 760$):

```{list-table}
:header-rows: 1

* - EDGE/VERTEX degree
  - 6
  - 7
  - 11
  - 16
  - 20
  - 24
* - $\max|Z - Z^\mathsf T|/\max|Z|$
  - 5.2e-3
  - 3.9e-3
  - 1.3e-3
  - 5.2e-4
  - 2.5e-4
  - 1.3e-4
```

Degree 16 is the lowest that brings it under $10^{-3}$, the tolerance `RECIPROCITY_TOL` of
`solver/cma/z_matrix_diagnostics.py`. On that mesh the extra cost was measured as negligible
(13.3 s against 13.0 s for the GPU fill), because EDGE/VERTEX pairs are few. The production default
`"full_sym"` then symmetrises the matrix (see {doc}`galerkin-mom`).

## In this solver

:::{admonition} Theory vs. implementation
:class: theory

The parameters of the scheme, where they live and how to change them.
:::

```{list-table}
:header-rows: 1
:widths: 22 18 60

* - Argument of `impmet.impedance_matrix_z`
  - Default
  - Meaning
* - `quad_order`
  - 6
  - degree of the rule on both triangles of FAR pairs
* - `near_order`
  - `None` → `max(quad_order, 16)`
  - degree on both triangles of EDGE/VERTEX pairs (`NEAR_ORDER_DEFAULT = 16`)
* - `self_outer_order`
  - `None` → `quad_order`
  - degree on the test triangle of SELF pairs
* - `duffy_order`
  - 7
  - Gauss–Legendre points per direction of the Duffy integral (3 sub-triangles)
* - `fill_mode`
  - `"full_sym"`
  - see {doc}`galerkin-mom`
* - `only_categories`
  - `None`
  - diagnostic: keep only some categories, to attribute an error to one scheme
```

`rwg3.calculate_z_matrice` and the high-level algorithms use these defaults; changing an order means
calling the `impmet` assemblers directly. The same arguments exist on `impedance_matrix_z_sweep` and
`impedance_matrix_z_sweep_gpu`, which must stay consistent with `impedance_matrix_z` (their
regression tests check it). The script `solver/utils/quadrature/outer_order_study.py` reproduces the
convergence measurements behind the defaults.

**Modules.** `quadrature/singularity_classifier.py` (categories), `quadrature/gauss_dunavant.py`
(rules and mapping), `quadrature/duffy.py` (Duffy nodes), `quadrature/analytic_singular.py`
({eq}`eq-wilton`, scalar and batched versions).

**Tests.** `solver/utils/quadrature/tests/`: `test_gauss_dunavant.py`, `test_duffy.py`,
`test_analytic_singular.py`, `test_singularity_classifier.py`, `test_impedance_outer_order.py`,
`test_impedance_quadrature_normalisation.py`; and `test/test_impedance_non_coplanar.py`.

## Assumptions and limits

- **SELF ceiling.** With Duffy at order 7 the SELF integrals stay around $10^{-3}$ relative accuracy;
  raising only one of the two orders can make it worse.
- **Current term near singular pairs** is integrated with the plain degree-16 rule (no extraction).
  The code comment justifies it by the factor $(kh)^2/4$, which holds only while the cells stay small
  compared with the wavelength.
- **Flat triangles only.** The closed form {eq}`eq-wilton` and the rules assume planar triangles,
  which is what the mesh provides.
- The categories are decided from vertex indices: two triangles that touch geometrically but do not
  share vertex indices (duplicated nodes) are treated as FAR.
