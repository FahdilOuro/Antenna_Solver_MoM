# RWG basis functions

{.mom-status}
{bdg-info}`Draft` Written from `solver/rwg/rwg1.py`, `solver/rwg/rwg2.py` and `solver/rwg/rwg5.py` at commit `7c348a7`, awaiting review.

The Rao–Wilton–Glisson (RWG) functions {footcite:p}`rao1982` are the building blocks in which the
solver writes the unknown surface current. Every other stage (the impedance matrix, the excitation
vector, the currents and the fields) is expressed in them.

## The idea

A triangulated metal surface carries a current that we do not know. Instead of looking for the
current at every point, we look for **one number per interior edge**: how much current crosses that
edge.

Each interior edge $n$ is shared by two triangles. The basis function $\mathbf f_n$ lives only on
that pair: it flows out of one triangle, $T_n^+$, across the shared edge, and into the other one,
$T_n^-$. Everywhere else it is zero.

Two consequences make this choice work well:

- the current crossing edge $n$ is the same seen from either triangle, so the expansion has **no
  jump of normal current** between triangles, hence no spurious line charge along the edges;
- what leaves $T_n^+$ arrives in $T_n^-$: each function carries **two equal and opposite charges**,
  spread uniformly over its two triangles.

## Definition

Let $\ell_n$ be the length of edge $n$, $A_n^\pm$ the areas of $T_n^\pm$ and $\mathbf v_n^\pm$ the
vertex of $T_n^\pm$ that is **not** on edge $n$ (its *free vertex*). Following
{footcite:t}`rao1982`:

```{math}
:label: eq-rwg-def
\mathbf f_n(\mathbf r) =
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\ell_n}{2A_n^+}\,\boldsymbol\rho_n^+(\mathbf r), & \mathbf r \in T_n^+,\\[6pt]
\dfrac{\ell_n}{2A_n^-}\,\boldsymbol\rho_n^-(\mathbf r), & \mathbf r \in T_n^-,\\[6pt]
\mathbf 0, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
```

with $\boldsymbol\rho_n^+ = \mathbf r - \mathbf v_n^+$ and
$\boldsymbol\rho_n^- = \mathbf v_n^- - \mathbf r$. Both point in the direction of the current, from
$T_n^+$ towards $T_n^-$: away from $\mathbf v_n^+$, towards $\mathbf v_n^-$.

The surface current is approximated by a weighted sum over the $N$ interior edges:

```{math}
:label: eq-rwg-expansion
\mathbf J(\mathbf r) \approx \sum_{n=1}^{N} I_n\,\mathbf f_n(\mathbf r).
```

Finding the $N$ coefficients $I_n$ is what the Method of Moments does (see {doc}`galerkin-mom`).

## Properties

**Unit normal component across edge $n$.** On edge $n$, the component of $\boldsymbol\rho_n^+$
normal to the edge is the height $h_n^+$ of $T_n^+$ drawn from $\mathbf v_n^+$, and
$2A_n^+ = \ell_n h_n^+$. The normal component of $\mathbf f_n$ is therefore exactly 1 on both sides
of the edge. The current is continuous across the edge, and $I_n$ is the normal component of
$\mathbf J$ there (in A/m). The total current crossing the edge is $I_n \ell_n$ (in A).

**No normal component on the other four edges.** On an edge of $T_n^+$ that contains
$\mathbf v_n^+$, $\boldsymbol\rho_n^+$ is parallel to the edge. No current leaves the pair through
its outer boundary.

**Constant divergence.**

```{math}
:label: eq-rwg-div
\nabla_s\cdot\mathbf f_n(\mathbf r) =
\begin{cases}
+\ell_n / A_n^+, & \mathbf r \in T_n^+,\\
-\ell_n / A_n^-, & \mathbf r \in T_n^-,\\
0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
```

With the continuity equation $\nabla_s\cdot\mathbf J = -j\omega\,\rho_s$ (time convention
$e^{j\omega t}$, the one used throughout the solver), the charge carried by $T_n^+$ is
$-I_n\ell_n/(j\omega)$ and the charge carried by $T_n^-$ is $+I_n\ell_n/(j\omega)$. Their sum is
zero.

## In this solver

:::{admonition} Theory vs. implementation
:class: theory

Everything above is the general RWG theory. This section describes what the code actually does,
including its own conventions and choices.
:::

### Building the basis

`Triangles.get_edges()` ({mod}`solver.rwg.rwg1`) lists every edge of every triangle and groups the
triangles that share it.

- **One basis function per pair of triangles sharing an edge.** An edge shared by $k$ triangles
  produces one function for each of the $k(k-1)/2$ pairs. This is how junctions between several
  surfaces are represented.
- **Orientation.** In each pair, $T_n^+$ is the triangle that comes first in the triangle list
  (smaller index). The choice is arbitrary: reversing it only changes the sign of $I_n$.
- **Boundary edges** (one triangle) carry no basis function. The normal current is zero there,
  which is the correct condition on the free edge of a metal sheet.
- **Junction filter.** `filter_complexes_jonctions()` then looks at edges shared by exactly three
  triangles (three pairs). It removes the pairs whose two triangles carry the same label in the
  fourth row of the triangle array (`t[3]`, the surface tag written by
  `solver.utils.gmsh_function.extract_msh_to_mat`). Edges shared by four triangles or more are left
  as they are.

The lengths and areas come from `Edges.compute_edges_length()` and
`Triangles.calculate_triangles_area_and_center()`. `radiation_algorithm()` calls them in this order:
`get_edges` → `filter_complexes_jonctions` → `compute_edges_length`.

### The ρ vectors at the centroids

{py:class}`solver.rwg.rwg2.Vecteurs_Rho` stores, for every edge, $\boldsymbol\rho$ at the centroids:

```python
vecteur_rho_plus  = triangles_center[:, triangles_plus] - free_vertex_plus    # ρ_n^{c+} = r_n^{c+} − v_n^+
vecteur_rho_minus = free_vertex_minus - triangles_center[:, triangles_minus]  # ρ_n^{c−} = v_n^− − r_n^{c−}
```

These centroid values are used wherever a one-point rule per triangle is enough:

```{list-table}
:header-rows: 1
:widths: 34 66

* - Where
  - What it computes with ρ at the centroids
* - `rwg5.calculate_current_density`
  - the current density at the centroid of each triangle,
    $\mathbf J(\mathbf r^c) = \sum_n I_n\,\ell_n\,\boldsymbol\rho_n^{c\pm}/(2A_n^\pm)$, i.e.
    {eq}`eq-rwg-expansion` evaluated at the centroids
* - `rwg4.calculate_current_scattering`
  - the plane-wave right-hand side
    $V_m = \tfrac{\ell_m}{2}\big[\mathbf E^{\mathrm{inc}}(\mathbf r_m^{c+})\cdot\boldsymbol\rho_m^{c+} + \mathbf E^{\mathrm{inc}}(\mathbf r_m^{c-})\cdot\boldsymbol\rho_m^{c-}\big]$,
    the centroid rule applied to $\int \mathbf f_m\cdot\mathbf E^{\mathrm{inc}}\,dS$
* - `gap_source.multiple_gap_sources`
  - the gap-source excitation, from the projection of $\boldsymbol\rho_m^{c\pm}$ on the
    excitation axis (see {doc}`excitations`)
```

The **impedance matrix does not use the centroid values.** `solver.utils.impmet` evaluates
$\mathbf r - \mathbf v_n^\pm$ at every Gauss–Dunavant node of every triangle, with the sign of
$T_n^-$ carried by the product of edge signs (see {doc}`numerical-integration`).

For the fields, `solver.utils.dipole_parameters.compute_dipole_center_moment` replaces each basis
function by a small dipole at the middle of its two centroids, with moment
$\mathbf m_n = \ell_n I_n\,(\mathbf r_n^{c-} - \mathbf r_n^{c+})$ (see {doc}`radiated-fields`).

The preprocessing (edge list, search for $T_n^\pm$, ρ vectors at the centroids) follows the MATLAB
scripts of Makarov {footcite:p}`makarov2001,makarov2002`, from which `rwg1.py` and `rwg2.py` were
translated.

## Assumptions and limits

- **Infinitely thin conductors.** RWG functions on a triangulated surface model a current sheet of
  zero thickness. A thick conductor must be meshed as a closed surface.
- **The mesh must be conforming.** Two triangles are connected only if they share both vertices of
  an edge, by index. A vertex lying in the middle of a neighbour's edge (a hanging node), or two
  surfaces meshed separately with duplicated nodes along their common line, give **no basis
  function** there: the current cannot cross, as if the metal were cut.
- **Lowest order.** Inside a triangle the expansion {eq}`eq-rwg-expansion` is linear and its
  divergence {eq}`eq-rwg-div` is constant, so the charge is piecewise constant. Accuracy comes from
  mesh density. The solver's helper `mesh_and_gap_parameters` uses λ/20 by default (see
  {doc}`../user-guide/geometry-and-meshing`).
- **Junction filter.** The removal rule above applies only to edges shared by exactly three
  triangles, and depends on the surface labels written in `t[3]`.
- **Sign of $I_n$** depends on the arbitrary $T_n^\pm$ choice. Compare edge currents only between
  runs on the same mesh.
