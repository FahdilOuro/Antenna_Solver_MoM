# Choosing the mesh size

{.mom-status}
{bdg-info}`Draft` Every number on this page comes from `documentation/figure_notebooks/guide_mesh_size.ipynb` (solver commit `4282bf8`), which runs the solver on the meshes described. Awaiting review.

How fine the mesh must be depends on what varies where: the current along the antenna, the current
across a wide conductor, the field in the feed gap, and the coupling between conductors that are
close together. This page gives rules for each, measured with the solver on strip antennas, and two
gmsh recipes. The quantity watched is the input impedance $Z_\mathrm{in}$, the most mesh-sensitive
result of a radiation simulation; patterns and directivity converge faster.

## The rules

```{list-table}
:header-rows: 1
:widths: 34 66

* - Rule
  - Why (measured below)
* - **Make the edges of the feed gap mesh lines**
  - With gmsh, draw the gap as its own surface and glue it with `fragment`. If the gap edges cut
    through triangles, $R_\mathrm{in}$ of a half-wave dipole came out anywhere between 63 and 182 Ω
    depending on the element size, instead of 72 Ω.
* - **Make the gap at least as wide as the conductor**
  - The gap source feeds a cube of side $W$ around the feed point. On a strip wider than $W$, the
    edge cells are not fed: $R_\mathrm{in}$ fell from 79 Ω to 1.2 Ω.
* - **Put at least 3 cells in the gap**
  - The rule of `mesh_and_gap_parameters`. With aligned gap edges, 3, 6 or 12 cells in the gap give
    the same $Z_\mathrm{in}$ (within 0.2 Ω).
* - **Along the antenna, about λ/20 for a first look, λ/40 to λ/60 for numbers**
  - The input impedance of a half-wave dipole is within 3.7 % of the finest mesh at λ/20, 1.7 % at
    λ/40, 0.8 % at λ/60. The difference roughly halves when the cell length halves.
* - **Across a wide conductor, 4 cells graded towards the edges**
  - On a 0.04 λ wide strip, 1 cell is already within 1.5 Ω of 8 cells, but 2 uniform cells are 6 Ω
    off; 4 graded cells are within 1.1 Ω. Conductor losses need the edge grading more (see
    {doc}`../theory/lossy-conductors`).
* - **Near a conductor at distance $s$, cells of about $s/3$**
  - With a parallel strip 5 mm away, the input impedance differs by 9 % from the finest mesh with
    cells as long as the spacing, and by 2 % with cells of $0.3\,s$.
* - **Refine locally, not everywhere**
  - Fine cells around the feed and λ/10 elsewhere gave $R_\mathrm{in}$ within 2.4 Ω of the finest
    uniform mesh with 44 unknowns instead of 297.
* - **Below λ/100, ask why**
  - The cost grows quickly and the matrix becomes less well conditioned
    ({doc}`../theory/numerical-limits`); the results above do not need it.
* - **Check by halving**
  - Run once more with all cell sizes halved. With the slow, first-order convergence measured below,
    the change is about the remaining error of the finer run, and half that of the coarser one.
```

These figures are for strip antennas at their first resonance. The rules carry over to other
shapes, but the numbers will not be the same: check with the last rule.

### Along the antenna

The current of a dipole varies along its length on the scale of the wavelength. On the structured
half-wave dipole of panel (a), with the gap unchanged:

| cells per wavelength | 10 | 20 | 40 | 60 | 80 | 120 (reference) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $Z_\mathrm{in}$ (Ω) | 69.9 − 2.9j | 71.1 − 0.9j | 71.8 + 0.4j | 72.1 + 1.0j | 72.2 + 1.2j | 72.3 + 1.5j |
| difference | 6.9 % | 3.7 % | 1.7 % | 0.8 % | 0.4 % | |

The resistance settles first; the reactance, which sets the resonant frequency, keeps moving by a few
ohms. The convergence is slow, roughly first order: halving the cells halves the difference. The
reference is therefore not exact either: going from λ/60 to λ/120 still changed $Z_\mathrm{in}$ by
0.8 %, about its own remaining error, so the differences in the table understate the true errors by
about that much. This is also why halving the cells is a usable check.

### Across a wide conductor

The current on a strip is largest at its edges. For the input impedance of a 40 mm strip fed over its
whole width (panel b), the number of cells across matters little: 80.3 + 3.8j Ω with 1 cell,
81.0 + 2.5j Ω with 8 cells graded towards the edges (`edge_grading="cosine"` in
`solver.optim.geometries.planar`). 2 uniform cells are the worst case (82.9 + 8.0j Ω). Use 4 cells,
graded. Losses are a different matter: the loss in the metal concentrates at the edges and keeps
changing as the edge cells shrink, which is why the reduced multiport model calibrates its loss factor
for one fixed rule, 4 graded cells ({doc}`../theory/reduced-multiport`).

### The feed gap

The gap source of `radiation_algorithm` ({doc}`../theory/excitations`) applies the field $V/W$ to
every triangle whose **centroid** lies in a cube of side $W$ centred on the feed point. Two
consequences, measured:

- **On a conductor wider than $W$**, the triangles near its edges are outside the cube and are not
  fed. Panel (c): on a 40 mm strip, a 30 mm gap gives 78.7 Ω with 1 cell across (all centroids inside)
  and 1.2 Ω with 8 cells; a 50 mm gap gives 79 Ω whatever the number of cells.
- **When the gap edges cut through triangles**, a triangle is fed entirely or not at all according to
  where its centroid falls, so the fed length, and with it the applied voltage, changes from one mesh
  to the next. Panel (e): the same dipole meshed by gmsh as a single rectangle gives
  80, 182, 77 and 63 Ω at λ/20, λ/30, λ/60 and λ/120. With the gap drawn as its own surface, the gap
  edges are mesh lines and the same four meshes give 71.6 to 73.0 Ω, in agreement with the structured
  mesh. Once the gap edges are aligned, the number of cells in the gap no longer matters: 3, 6 and 12
  cells gave 72.40 + 1.75j, 72.39 + 1.55j and 72.39 + 1.56j Ω.

`check_simulation_stability` does not detect a misaligned gap: it checks the ratio of the gap width
to the element size, not where the gap edges fall.

### Conductors close together

A conductor at distance $s$ changes the current on its neighbour over lengths of order $s$. Panel (d):
a strip with a parallel strip 5 mm away (λ/60) converges more slowly than the same strip alone, and
not monotonically. With cells as long as the spacing the input impedance differs by 9 % from the
finest mesh tried, against 3 % for the strip alone; with cells of $0.3\,s$, 2 % against 0.5 %. The
finest mesh ($h = 0.22\,s$) is the reference, so these differences understate the true error.
Strongly coupled configurations with a small input resistance (here 4.7 Ω) are the most sensitive.

## gmsh recipes

Both are taken from the notebook above, where they are run on the dipole of
{doc}`../getting-started/first-simulation` (`WID4`, `LEN4`, `GAP4` are its width, length and gap).

**The gap as its own surface.** Draw the strip and the gap region, and glue them with `fragment`:
the gap edges become mesh lines.

```python
strip = gmsh.model.occ.addRectangle(-WID4 / 2, -LEN4 / 2, 0, WID4, LEN4)
gap = gmsh.model.occ.addRectangle(-WID4 / 2, -GAP4 / 2, 0, WID4, GAP4)
gmsh.model.occ.fragment([(2, strip)], [(2, gap)])
gmsh.model.occ.synchronize()
```

The mesh then has several surfaces, and the 4th row of `t` carries their gmsh tags.
`radiation_algorithm` uses all of them. `scattering_algorithm` keeps only the triangles labelled 1
(see {doc}`scattering`).

**Fine cells around the feed only.** A gmsh `Ball` size field sets the element size inside a sphere
around the feed point; `generate_and_save_mesh` sets the size everywhere else:

```python
ball = gmsh.model.mesh.field.add("Ball")
for key, value in (("XCenter", 0.0), ("YCenter", 0.0), ("ZCenter", 0.0), ("Radius", GAP4),
                   ("VIn", fine_near_gap), ("VOut", size)):
    gmsh.model.mesh.field.setNumber(ball, key, value)
gmsh.model.mesh.field.setAsBackgroundMesh(ball)
generate_and_save_mesh(path, size)
```

With the gap aligned, λ/10 elsewhere and $W/3$ near the feed, the dipole has 44 unknowns and
$Z_\mathrm{in}$ = 70.6 − 5.2j Ω; λ/20 elsewhere, 60 unknowns, 71.0 − 3.7j Ω; uniform λ/120, 297
unknowns, 73.0 + 5.5j Ω. The resistance agrees within 2.4 Ω. The reactance differs by up to 10 Ω
between these gmsh meshes: it depends on how the triangles are laid across the 10 mm width, which a
single size does not control.

The solver also has an adaptive refinement loop that refines where the solution asks for it
({bdg-warning-line}`Experimental`, `solver/utils/h_refinement_loop.py`); see
{doc}`geometry-and-meshing`.

## The helpers

`gmsh_function.mesh_and_gap_parameters(frequency, resolution=20, gap_fraction=0.15, n_edges_min=3)`
returns an element size of λ/`resolution` and a gap of `gap_fraction` × λ, and makes the element
smaller if fewer than `n_edges_min` elements would fit in the gap. It does not make the gap edges mesh
lines (draw the gap as above) and does not know the width of the conductor (keep $W$ at least as
wide). `check_simulation_stability` checks, after a run, the input resistance range, the gap to
element ratio and the element size against λ/10 and λ/100.
